3. BRIGHTNESS TEMPERATURE AND OPTICAL DEPTH
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Planck brightness temperature at 225.2 GHz from the azimuthal mean; T_d = (phi L* / 8 pi r^2 sigma_SB)^(1/4) with phi = 0.020, L* = 30.00 Lsun, floor 10 K.
  tau = -ln(1 - T_b/T_d), absorption only. T_b is beam-averaged, so it is a lower bound for features narrower than the beam.

  No rings or gaps were fitted or detected, so there are no radii to evaluate.

  Interpretation: tau well below 1 means the emission is optically thin and the flux traces the dust surface density directly;
  tau of order 1 or more means a column that is partly or fully optically thick. Scattering lowers T_b below the
  absorption-only value, so the true optical depth of strongly scattering grains is higher than tau here.

5. RING WIDTHS, DUST TRAPPING AND RING SPACING
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A dust ring in a Gaussian gas pressure bump of width w has width w_d = w sqrt(alpha / (alpha + St)), i.e. alpha / St = 1 / ((w / w_d)^2 - 1) (Dullemond et al. 2018, DSHARP VI).
  w_d = Gaussian sigma of the fitted ring with the beam removed. A long-lived bump cannot be narrower than about one gas scale height H (passive-disk temperature, M* = 2.17 Msun), so w = H gives the LARGEST alpha / St the ring allows;
  a bump as wide as the distance to the nearest gap gives the smallest. A ring wider than H gives no limit: the bump could be as wide as the ring.

    r [au]   sigma_d  H [au]   sd/H | alpha/St max (w=H)   min (w=w_max) | St (alpha=1e-3)     St (alpha=1e-4)      notes

  alpha/St: ratio of the turbulent diffusion parameter to the Stokes number of the trapped grains (smaller = stronger trapping). The range in the 'St' columns is alpha divided by the two limits,
  for the two alpha values of section 4; '>=' means no upper limit (the nearest gap is not wider than H). The ring width is the intensity width: for optically thick rings it overestimates the dust-density width,
  so the true rings are, if anything, narrower (stronger trapping). Blended components (see the warning in section 1) and rings with large errors are unreliable; trust the isolated rings.

  Spacing of the gaps (planets sit in the gaps): radius ratio, orbital period ratio (r2/r1)^(3/2) and the nearest low-order commensurability:
  fewer than two gaps: nothing to compare.
