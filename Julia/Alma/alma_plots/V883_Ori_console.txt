  Model: tapered power law + 1 Gaussian ring(s); chi2_red = 2.36 (dof 59). Errors are 1 sigma, scaled by sqrt(chi2_red) when > 1.
  Per-bin errors: image noise / sqrt(independent beams) plus 2% of the local flux.
  Fit range: r < 385 au only. Beyond it the profile is significantly negative (negative bowl: spatial filtering removes extended emission), which no disc model describes; including it would inflate chi2 and the errors. Rings or gaps beyond that radius are not fitted.
  Smooth disk: amplitude 7.8 mJy/beam, characteristic radius 31 au, power-law index 0.30

   #  r [au]          amp [mJy/beam]      S/N  FWHM_int [au]      FWHM/r              share  seed      
   1  69.0 +/- 3.6    0.948 +/- 0.061    15.6  72.4 +/- 3.9         1.05                88%  detected  

  FWHM_int: observed Gaussian FWHM with the radial beam (sigma 4.6 au) subtracted in quadrature; '<' marks widths consistent with the beam (upper limit at +1 sigma).
  share: fraction of the local azimuthal-mean flux (at the ring radius) that this ring component supplies.

  Fit decisions (in order):
    - restricted to r < 385 au (negative bowl beyond it: spatial filtering, not emission)
    - starting seeds: 73.5 au (detected)
  WARNING: the smooth disk collapsed to the unresolved core (characteristic radius 31 au < 3 beams): the extended emission is shared among the ring components, so overlapping neighbours (shoulders) have degenerate widths and amplitudes; trust the isolated rings, not the blended ones.

----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Fourier amplitudes around each ring (window = +/- one observed sigma, at least half the minor axis), relative to the fitted ring amplitude (the table after this one gives them as a fraction of the local flux).
  FAP = false-alarm probability exp(-S/N^2/2) for one ring (floored at 1e-6), for pure noise only; with N rings expect N x FAP chance detections. Geometry errors are NOT in the noise.

  r =   69.0 au: m=1 at 7.7 sigma, m=2 at 3.4 sigma; maximum within 25 deg of the minor axis (near/far-side brightness asymmetry?)

  The same amplitudes as a fraction of the local azimuthal-mean flux (comparable between rings and between targets):
    r [au] | local mean [mJy/beam] | m=1 [% of flux] | m=2 [% of flux]
      69.0 | 1.13               | 4.6 +/- 0.6    | 2.0 +/- 0.6   
  NOTE: the feature(s) at 69 au are shoulders (the raw-profile peak finder finds no peak or dip within 1 beam): an amplitude measured against a shoulder baseline can be large without a real azimuthal structure; the percentages of the local flux are the safer numbers.

  Elliptical-beam check for m=2: an axisymmetric ring convolved with a beam that is not round has a higher peak where the ring runs along the
  beam's short axis, i.e. a pure m=2 pattern with no real structure. Expectation for each ring (fitted intrinsic width) versus what is measured;
  excess = size of the vector difference between the measured and the expected m=2 term (formal S/N, noise only):
    r [au] | beam alone: % @ az | measured: % @ az   | excess [%] (S/N)
      69.0 | 0.2 @ 164 deg      | 2.4 @ 84 deg       | 2.6 (3.7 s)
  Azimuths are disk-plane angles from the major axis (mod 180). The expectation uses the same window-mean estimator as the measurement (the m=2 of the ring PEAK is ~1.65x larger).

  Geometry sensitivity of m=2 (first-order estimate, not a correction): an inclination error of 3 deg alone moves the deprojected radius by (cos i / cos(i + 3 deg) - 1)/2 x cos(2 phi), which on a profile with logarithmic slope S gives an m=2 of |S| times that:
           |          |  m=2 from a 3 deg error [%] |            measured m=2 [%]
    r [au] |        S |  of ring amp.       of flux |  of ring amp.       of flux
      69.0 |    -0.75 |           2.1           1.8 |           2.4           2.0
  Each quantity is given relative to the ring amplitude used in the table above and as a fraction of the local azimuthal-mean flux ('n/a': the ring amplitude is below 10% of the local flux). A measured m=2 of the same size as the estimate is not distinguishable from a small inclination error unless the ridge geometry fit (section 1b) or a second ring settles it.

  The same for a position-angle error of 3 deg: the deprojected radius moves by (eps/2) sin^2(i)/cos(i) x sin(2 phi) (eps in rad), an m=2 pattern with its maximum 45 deg from the major axis (the inclination pattern above peaks ON the axes):
           |          |     m=2 from a PA error [%]
    r [au] |        S |  of ring amp.       of flux
      69.0 |    -0.75 |           1.3           1.1
  Not included: ring curvature at small r, non-Gaussian ring profiles, CLEAN / uv-coverage effects (the vertical-thickness scan is opt-in: do_thickness = true).

  Round-beam test: switched off (do_roundbeam = false).

  Coherence across rings (a vortex sits at one radius; a centre / inclination / PA error shows at every ring with the same orientation):
    fewer than 2 rings with a significant amplitude: no coherence test.

  Candidate arcs / clumps (beam-smoothed residual above 4.0 sigma of the smoothed residual, >= 1 beam of area, outside the 29 au exclusion zone):
    none
    2 further region(s) lie inside the 29 au exclusion zone, where ring detection is also disabled (beam / CLEAN / centre effects): + at 13 au, S/N 13.8, span 332 deg; - at 26 au, S/N 8.7, span 89 deg. They are not tabulated.
    2 further region(s) above the threshold lie outside the disc emission (the azimuthal mean there is below 3 sigma): + at 416 au, S/N 4.3, span 2 deg; + at 369 au, S/N 4.5, span 3 deg. They are not disc features (more likely noise or background sources) and are not tabulated.

  How to read this: a single significant positive arc covering a limited azimuth range at a ring radius (span of tens of degrees,
  contrast tens of percent) is the signature of a dust trap such as a vortex. A +/- pair 180 deg apart (in the residual image, not the ring-averaged amplitudes)
  is what a small centre or inclination error produces.
  The noise estimate for the arcs is the robust scatter of the smoothed residual itself, so real structure inflates it: the threshold is conservative.
