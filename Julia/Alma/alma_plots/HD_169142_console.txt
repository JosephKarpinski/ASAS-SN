3. BRIGHTNESS TEMPERATURE AND OPTICAL DEPTH
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Planck brightness temperature at 338.2 GHz from the azimuthal mean; T_d = (phi L* / 8 pi r^2 sigma_SB)^(1/4) with phi = 0.020, L* = 8.00 Lsun, floor 10 K.
  tau = -ln(1 - T_b/T_d), absorption only. T_b is beam-averaged, so it is a lower bound for features narrower than the beam.

  type     r [au] | T_b [K]           T_d [K] T_b/T_d     tau      H/r

  gap        14.3 | 13.4 +/- 0.5         56.9    0.24    0.27    0.043

  Interpretation: tau well below 1 means the emission is optically thin and the flux traces the dust surface density directly;

  absorption-only value, so the true optical depth of strongly scattering grains is higher than tau here.

4. GAP-OPENING PLANET MASSES
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Gap depth and width are measured on the azimuthal-mean profile (Zhang et al. 2018 definitions: next ring peak outside the gap, edge level = (peak + minimum)/2).
  Width estimate: Kanagawa et al. (2016) Delta_gap/Rp = 0.41 (Mp/M*)^(1/2) (h/r)^(-3/4) alpha^(-1/4), i.e. Mp/M* = 2.1e-3 (Delta_gap/Rp)^2 (h/r / 0.05)^(3/2) (alpha/1e-3)^(1/2).
  Depth estimate: Kanagawa et al. (2015) Sigma_min/Sigma_0 = 1/(1 + 0.04 K), K = (Mp/M*)^2 (h/r)^(-5) alpha^(-1), with Sigma_0/Sigma_min taken as the dust-intensity contrast.
  (Zhang et al. 2018 give dust-specific calibrations that also depend on grain size; they are not applied here, so the dust gaps are read with the gas relations.)
  M* = 1.65 Msun; h/r from the passive-disk T_d at each gap (H = c_s/v_K r). Both alpha values are shown because the mass scales roughly as alpha^(1/2) (width) and alpha^(1/2) (depth).

     gap    r_c    h/r  depth | W_obs W_int    W/r | width a=1e-3       width a=1e-4       | depth a=1e-3       depth a=1e-4        notes
    14.3   13.6  0.043   2.39 |  16.1  15.6  1.184 | 3.9-4.1 Mjup       1.2-1.3 Mjup       | 2.4 Mnep           0.74 Mnep           inner ring peak below the edge level (width = 2 x outer half-width)

  Not evaluated: 34 au: no local maximum outward of the gap minimum at 34.4 au (the profile does not rise there)

  Columns: gap = radius you asked for, r_c = centre of the measured gap; depth = I(ring peak outside) / I(gap minimum); W_obs / W_int = gap width at the edge level, observed and with the beam (geometric-mean FWHM 3.8 au) removed in quadrature; W/r = observed width over the gap centre.
  The width range runs from the deconvolved to the observed width. Treat the numbers as order-of-magnitude: alpha is unknown (the two values bracket the usual range),
  h/r comes from an assumed passive-disk temperature, the width relation was calibrated for gas gaps (dust gaps from large grains are wider, so masses are upper estimates),
  the depth is diluted by the beam (lower bound on the contrast) but dust gaps are deeper than gas gaps (upper bound on the mass), and several gaps may be carved by one planet or by other mechanisms.

5. RING WIDTHS, DUST TRAPPING AND RING SPACING
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  A dust ring in a Gaussian gas pressure bump of width w has width w_d = w sqrt(alpha / (alpha + St)), i.e. alpha / St = 1 / ((w / w_d)^2 - 1) (Dullemond et al. 2018, DSHARP VI).
  w_d = Gaussian sigma of the fitted ring with the beam removed. A long-lived bump cannot be narrower than about one gas scale height H (passive-disk temperature, M* = 1.65 Msun), so w = H gives the LARGEST alpha / St the ring allows;
  a bump as wide as the distance to the nearest gap gives the smallest. A ring wider than H gives no limit: the bump could be as wide as the ring.

    r [au]   sigma_d  H [au]   sd/H | alpha/St max (w=H)   min (w=w_max) | St (alpha=1e-3)     St (alpha=1e-4)      notes
      11.2 6.2+/-2.0     0.5  13.39 | no limit             -            | -                   -                    ring at least as wide as H
      25.5 3.7+/-0.2     1.3   2.83 | no limit             -            | -                   -                    ring at least as wide as H
      61.4 3.9+/-1.0     3.9   1.00 | 331.02 (>1.27)       0.021        | 3.0e-06-4.7e-02     3.0e-07-4.7e-03      

  alpha/St: ratio of the turbulent diffusion parameter to the Stokes number of the trapped grains (smaller = stronger trapping). The range in the 'St' columns is alpha divided by the two limits,
  for the two alpha values of section 4; '>=' means no upper limit (the nearest gap is not wider than H). The ring width is the intensity width: for optically thick rings it overestimates the dust-density width,
  so the true rings are, if anything, narrower (stronger trapping). Blended components (see the warning in section 1) and rings with large errors are unreliable; trust the isolated rings.

  Spacing of the gaps (planets sit in the gaps): radius ratio, orbital period ratio (r2/r1)^(3/2) and the nearest low-order commensurability (measured gaps only, at their measured centres r_c):
  fewer than two gaps: nothing to compare.

CAVEATS: L*, M* are approximate and the passive-disk T_d ignores accretion heating and the surface-layer/midplane difference; 
the ring fits depend on the seed positions (see the 'seed' column); all errors are statistical only.
